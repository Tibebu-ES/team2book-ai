With [Teamup’s sub-calendars](https://www.teamup.com/getting-organized-with-sub-calendars/), color-coding is easy. Choose from a palette of 48 colors and assign the right one to each calendar. You can even use a CSV template to [import your color-coded calendar structure](https://calendar.teamup.com/kb/how-to-create-a-color-coded-calendar-structure-from-a-csv-file/)! When you color-code your calendar, you gain visual cues that can increase your efficiency. Your brain can quickly identify events based on color, at a glance. Plus, color-coding can create a more appealing calendar look, whether you prefer a bright rainbow or a minimal look.

### The benefits of color-coding

[Research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3743993/) shows that color enhances memory performance and is “as a powerful information channel to the human cognitive system.” We’re all overwhelmed by lots of information and tasks that demand our attention. Our brains sort through a huge amount of data every day, and most of this processing is [visual](https://www.northeastohioboomer.com/blogs/memory-enhancement-your-brain-on-color/).

Color works as a powerful cue that shortcuts processing time.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="width: 798px">[![](https://blog.teamup.com/wp-content/plugins/native-lazyload/assets/images/placeholder.svg)<noscript>![](https://blog.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/content-planning-teamup-table-view.png)</noscript>](https://blog.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/content-planning-teamup-table-view.png)<figcaption class="wp-caption-text">*Color-code across multiple dimensions with Teamup*</figcaption></figure>When you associate the color red with urgent, you’ll automatically start paying attention to the red-colored events on the calendar first.

You can use the same concept to automatically understand the context of any calendar event.

It may seem like a small effect, but consider that your brain is receiving, processing, and responding to enormous [amounts of data](https://heyday.xyz/blog/information-overload/) every single day. A shortcut that shaves off a few seconds of processing time, over and over, can result in lots of saved time over days and weeks. Plus, color recognition means less cognitive load, since it reduces the attention needed to sort and categorize each bit of information.

### Keys to color-coding like a pro

Okay, now that you know why it’s smart to color-code your calendar, let’s take a look at how to do it well.

The consistency makes it easier to keep things organized and get those quick visual cues. #### **Use** contrast

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="width: 550px">[![Sub-calendar inspiration organize by region or area](https://blog.teamup.com/wp-content/plugins/native-lazyload/assets/images/placeholder.svg)<noscript>![Sub-calendar inspiration organize by region or area](https://www.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/sub-calendar-inspiration-organize-by-branch.png)</noscript>](https://www.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/sub-calendar-inspiration-organize-by-branch.png)<figcaption class="wp-caption-text">*A good example of calendars with contrasting colors*</figcaption></figure>For maximum visual impact, choose contrasting colors. Dark and light colors, or warm and cool colors, will contrast with each other and make it easier to visually distinguish between each one.

#### Work with color families

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="width: 550px">[![sub-calendar inspiration organize by student and tutor](https://blog.teamup.com/wp-content/plugins/native-lazyload/assets/images/placeholder.svg)<noscript>![sub-calendar inspiration organize by student and tutor](https://www.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/sub-calendar-inspiration-after-school-tutoring-organization.png)</noscript>](https://www.teamup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/sub-calendar-inspiration-after-school-tutoring-organization.png)<figcaption class="wp-caption-text">*Use color families to distinguish between different calendar sets*</figcaption></figure>When you have a larger number of sub-calendars, color families can help with visual distinction. For example, perhaps you have 5 team members for Department A. All the calendars go into a Department A folder, and are assigned colors in the warm red-pink family. When you see an event in that color family, you automatically know it belongs to someone in Department A. For Department B, choose a contrasting color family (e.g., cool blues).

#### Color-code across dimensions

Perhaps you use calendar color-coding for one dimension (e.g. people), and you need a way to visually distinguish another dimension (e.g. task type, or location).

You can accomplish this with two sets of sub-calendars. Each set represents one dimension. Assign any event to the relevant sub-calendar from each set, for a [dual color-coded look](https://teamup.com/ksi2xk6ja4d9pr8ewz/events/1416412143) like this:
